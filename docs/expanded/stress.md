# Optional accumulating stress

These are **original optional campaign rules**, not rules from `full-2008`.
Include `vhyl-rpg-expanded-stress.t` after the expanded foundation, health and
traits. The all-module umbrella includes it, but no character accumulates stress
until an owned state is explicitly configured. Existing fear events never add
stress automatically.

Create a `RpgExpandedStressPolicy` and `RpgExpandedStressState`, set the state's
`profile` and `policy`, then call `configure()`. Configuration binds the policy;
changing it afterward rejects. Defaults are a 0–12 balance, explicit mild,
moderate and severe gains of 1, 2 and 3, and thresholds at 4, 8 and 12. Policy
fields can be changed before configuration. The balance is independent of
injury, fatigue, advancement points and lasting traits.

## Gains and decisions

A `RpgExpandedStressGain` supplies a unique `id`, unique `cause`, `state`,
`severity` and `evidence`. An optional `fearEvent` must be an already recorded
event owned by the same character. `assess()` is pure; `apply()` commits once.
A second request using the same cause cannot accumulate again. Receipts retain
`requested`, `applied` and `discarded`; overflow is discarded at the cap.

Crossed thresholds create ordered `RpgExpandedStressDecision` objects. Each
threshold fires once per episode. Returning to zero starts a new episode and
rearms thresholds without deleting old decisions. Resolve `state.pending()`
first with a `RpgExpandedStressResolution` and an evidenced choice:

- `coping`: record the response without a mechanical penalty.
- `strain`: apply −1 to contextual skill/attribute checks for 600 seconds.
  Further strain refreshes the effective duration without stacking its penalty.
  Self-control, damage and derived characteristics are unchanged.
- `fear`: supply a fresh owned `RpgExpandedFearEvent`. Resolve its ordinary
  check and follow-ups. If it selects a consequence, the resolution remains
  pending until `completeFear(consequence)` commits the normal owned consequence.
  Select the consequence in the event's ordinary same-time authoring window.
  No stress gain is generated by this fear event.

Pending decisions reject voluntary checks, shared challenge operations, attack
requests and non-recovery maneuvers before costs or dice. Passive defenses,
incoming injury, involuntary health checks and care/recovery remain available.
`Action.verify()` supplies the parser/structured-action gate for a bound player
profile. Custom verification overrides must call inherited verification.
Recovery and decision actions bind their actual `stressRecovery` or
`stressResolution` object; the Echo Vault example also validates the same owner
in its shared dialogue operation. Authors must use these gates for custom
activities; directly mutating world state is not a supported action adapter.

## Safe-hour recovery

`RpgExpandedStressRecovery` owns one safe, uninterrupted interval. Supply a
unique `id`, `state`, `safe = true` and factual `evidence`; call `begin()` before
advancing the existing clock. No second interval may overlap it. The shared
clock settles the interval at its one-hour boundary, even when a larger time
advance crosses that boundary. It credits the campaign day in which the hour
ends, at most four points per day. Additional hours need fresh owned intervals.

`interrupt()` retains a zero-credit receipt for an incomplete interval. Incoming
injury and new stress gains interrupt active rest. Ordinary authored player
actions interrupt it through the shared action check. Custom time-consuming
operations must interrupt the owned interval before committing unsafe work.
Replaying `complete()` cannot recover twice. Completed recovery does not remove
strain, fear episodes, lasting traits or pending decisions.

All state lives in ordinary persistent objects and uses existing transactions,
undo and exact-compiled-game saves. Invalid requests reject without a partial
commit. See the [completion checklist](fear-completion.md) for observed validation
and [Echo Vault](../../examples/echo-vault/README.md) for a connected playable use.
